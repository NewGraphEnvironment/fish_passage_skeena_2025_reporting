# fish_passage_skeena_2025_reporting 0.6.0 (2026-08-06)

* Name the spatial catalogue in the floodplain methods section rather than referring to it in the abstract. The section said the delineation was read from "the spatial catalogue" without saying which one, while the infrastructure that serves it is described two hundred lines later under aerial imagery — so a reader reaching the floodplain methods had nothing to go on. It now cites the catalogue software, cross-references the imagery section that describes the same stack, gives the item naming convention and the specific item this report reads, and points readers at the `stac_floodplains_bc` repository, where the publishing pipeline and instructions for querying the collection are documented. Both the methods section and the appendix link the repository rather than the collection's raw JSON endpoint, which is not much use to a reader.
* Correct the attribution of the stream network the floodplain delineation was built on. The methods section and appendix both credited it to `bcfishpass`. It was built with `link` over the BC Freshwater Atlas — a different pipeline — so the report was crediting inputs it had not verified. The appendix now also states that the stream lengths it reports are measured against the `bcfishpass` network used elsewhere in this report rather than the delineation's own, which is not published to the catalogue, and that the two are aligned by intent rather than by construction. The lengths are close estimates, not exact intersections.
* Move the floodplain detail map from north of Smithers to south of Smithers. The reach running south through Telkwa toward Quick carries far more agricultural conversion of the valley bottom, which is the land use the background chapter identifies as having altered flow regimes, water temperatures and bank stability along the Bulkley. The southern frame also brings the Telkwa River confluence and a second municipality into view.
* State what the program is for, in the executive summary and in the recommendations. Each field and modelling activity was described on its own, leaving the reader to infer why they belong in one report. Both chapters now open the same argument: the work answers one question — what condition are these watersheds in, and where will restoration effort do the most good — and each activity answers a different part of it. Passage assessments and the provincial model establish where fish can reach; habitat confirmations, fish sampling and environmental DNA establish where fish are; floodplain delineation and aerial imagery establish how infrastructure has altered the valley bottom; climate departure and water temperature establish the trajectory; effectiveness monitoring tests whether the work delivered. The summary also states that making the evidence usable — structured field forms, methods documented beside the code that implements them, layers published to open catalogues, reporting written to be questioned — is treated as part of the work rather than a by-product.
* Reorganise the recommendations from topic to purpose: extending the lines of evidence, testing whether the work delivers, making the knowledge usable, and partnerships and capacity. Sixteen recommendations rather than eighteen, with the web map, open data and community-of-practice items collected into one group instead of scattered across three unrelated ones.
* Add stream temperature and Growing-Season Degree Days to the methods and results chapters, and cite `water-temp-bc` ([Issue #16](https://github.com/NewGraphEnvironment/fish_passage_skeena_2025_reporting/issues/16)). The 2025 Bayesian Spatial Stream Network modelling with Poisson Consulting was carried out in this study area — it extended the earlier Nechako work to a larger network and added Growing-Season Degree Days as the productivity-relevant metric for age-0 salmonid growth — and this report did not mention it. Both studies are now linked in the water temperature attachment, and the observations behind them are credited to `water-temp-bc`, which had been referenced by link in other reports but never cited.
* Promote the statistical modelling content out of a fourth-level heading buried under Planning and into its own Methods section, matching the template and the Peace report. The heading no longer names `bcfishpass` alone, because the work now supports our own habitat modelling as well as the provincial outputs. Point the water temperature attachment links at `NewGraphEnvironment` rather than `poissonconsulting`.

# fish_passage_skeena_2025_reporting 0.2.0 (2026-08-05)

* Add a climate departure appendix for the Skeena. Long-term climate data for the five watershed groups is compared between a recent decade (2015-2025) and a pre-warming reference period (1951-1980), then broken down by ecoregion and mapped back onto the watershed groups that organise barrier prioritisation. The analysis was rebuilt for this study area rather than carried over; the version the Fraser report published covers a different part of the province ([Issue #3](https://github.com/NewGraphEnvironment/fish_passage_skeena_2025_reporting/issues/3)).
* Report the findings this study area actually shows, which differ from the Fraser's in two ways that matter. Warming is remarkably even across the Skeena: every watershed group sits within a quarter of a degree of every other, so climate departure gives no basis for ranking one group above another and the appendix says so. And the mountain ecoregions have warmed slightly faster than the interior plateau here, the reverse of the pattern the Fraser reports.
* Report the snowpack signal, which is the clearest result in the appendix. Snow is leaving the study area earlier rather than falling in smaller amounts: the melt midpoint has moved fifteen days earlier over the record, summer snowpack is down sixty per cent, and the melt that used to come in summer now arrives in spring. The freshet a crossing was designed against is arriving substantially earlier than the record it was sized from, and the late-summer cold water that snowpack used to provide is declining.
* Regenerate the stock assessment extract for Skeena waterbodies, which had been left as a placeholder ([Issue #6](https://github.com/NewGraphEnvironment/fish_passage_skeena_2025_reporting/issues/6)). Correcting a stream-name filter that had been pulling escapement records from a Haida Gwaii creek sharing a name with one of this season's sites, and a download link that had gone stale at the source.
* Correct two data attachment links that returned errors, and point the habitat assessment attachment at the PSCIS reassessment workbook this season actually produced.

# fish_passage_skeena_2025_reporting 0.1.0 (2026-08-04)

* First reviewable draft of the Skeena 2025 report. Spawned from `fish_passage_fraser_2025_reporting` at `7ef34cc` for its thematic appendix structure, eDNA appendix and effectiveness monitoring memo pattern, then retargeted to the Skeena throughout. The background chapter is ported from `fish_passage_skeena_2024_reporting`, which already carried the Wet'suwet'en, Gitxsan and Tsimshian sections and per-river hydrology for the Bulkley, Morice, Zymoetz, Kispiox and Kitsumkalum.
* Report the 2025 field season as what it was. This was a monitoring and environmental DNA season: two PSCIS reassessments, two effectiveness monitoring sites, one habitat confirmation record and twenty eDNA samples. No Phase 1 assessments and no Phase 2 habitat confirmations were completed, and the executive summary, results and recommendations say so rather than carrying forward a narrative built for a season with assessment data in it.
* Add monitoring appendices for both effectiveness monitoring sites. Peacock Creek carries a pre-remediation habitat confirmation rating both reaches high value, multi-pass electrofishing at three closed sites above and three below the culvert, and 2025 environmental DNA confirming the reach is occupied. Waterfall Creek documents a culvert removed outright by the Gitksan Watershed Authorities, with the riprap buried, seeded and regrowing.
* Present the effectiveness monitoring form results in both monitoring appendices. The metrics recorded in the field — velocity, substrate, constriction, riparian condition, stability, revegetation and cover — had been collected and were appearing nowhere in the report, because the memo pattern inherited from the Fraser report does not include the table. Same gap exists in Fraser and in the template, filed there.
* Report environmental DNA results as detected or not detected, matching the call convention the laboratory applies. The report had carried a third tier for results of 1-3 positive droplets. UNBC's method retests any sample returning 2-3 droplets and, where the additional droplets do not bring the total to four or more, calls it negative — so the tier described as sub-threshold is simply negative, and presenting it separately implied a result the analysis does not support.
* Set the modelled species to steelhead, following the 2024 Skeena report. This selects which bcfishpass habitat view is queried, the gradient thresholds applied to it, and the symbology on the site maps — it is not a labelling change.
* Handle a field season with no Phase 2 habitat confirmation sites. Three scripts assumed at least one existed and failed on the empty case with errors that named the symptom rather than the cause. This is template code and every monitoring-only season would have hit it.
* Stop the report from publishing another region's data. A spawned repository inherits every table its ancestor committed to `data/bcfishpass.sqlite`, and the tables written by scripts that `index.Rmd` never sources are the ones nobody re-runs. The UAV imagery table held Peace region records, the photo metadata table held the ancestor repository's image URLs, and the site watershed table held the ancestor's sites. All three are now either regenerated for this region or dropped, because an absent map layer is visibly missing where another region's data looks like an answer.
* Remove the climate departure, floodplain and stock assessment data products inherited from the Fraser rather than republishing them. Each is real work this report should carry, but every input was generated for a different area of interest. Tracked as issues #3, #2 and #6.
